Architectural staircase CAD design

Stair design coordinates floor-to-floor height, available floor area and circulation. CAD helps compare alternatives before construction details are finalized.

Launch CAD2D3D

Set out the geometry

Measure floor-to-floor height and the available footprint. Draw flights, landings and slab openings. Compare straight, L-shaped and U-shaped arrangements.

Plans and sections

Represent treads, direction of travel and levels. Separate steps, railings, dimensions and structure into layers. Cross-check the section against the plan.

Review the model

Explore the space in the 3D workspace where useful. Check headroom, railings and dimensions against local regulations; a CAD drawing is not a compliance certificate.

Worked geometry exercise: a two-flight stair

Suppose a schematic project has a floor-to-floor height of 2.80 m. Dividing that height into 16 equal rises gives a rise of 0.175 m. This arithmetic is a starting point, not a code-compliant specification. Choose a preliminary tread depth, lay out the two flights and landing, and check that the stair fits the available footprint.

Draw the section from the same level datums as the plan. Count the risers, measure the clear headroom beneath the slab, and verify that landing and railing geometry does not conflict with adjacent doors. Use the 3D workspace to review the relationship between flights and the slab opening. Keep construction details separate from the schematic model. Check local requirements for riser and tread dimensions, landings, headroom, guards and handrails before finalizing the design.
